The most common beginner mistake in buying a board is copying an expert's shortboard — and then you can't paddle, can't stand up, and you're still riding whitewater a month later. This guide lays out the board types, popular models, and use cases that actually matter for a beginner.

If you want to understand **how to judge whether a board fits you** (volume math, how to check a used board), pair this with the lesson: [Beginner Board Buying Guide](/en/lessons/board-choice).

## 1. Three iron rules for a beginner board

1. **Bigger, thicker, more stable.** More board means more float — easier paddling, earlier take-offs, and a steadier stance.
2. **Look at volume (liters) before looks.** Volume is the single number a beginner should care about most.
3. **Choose from softboard / longboard / funboard — skip the shortboard.** Shortboards have low volume and turn fast; they're for after you can stand and turn.

### Estimating volume (beginner reference)

As a beginner, aim for a board volume of roughly **body weight (kg) × 0.9–1.0 or more**. For example:

| Weight | Suggested volume (L) | Typical board |
|---|---|---|
| 50 kg | 45 L+ | Longboard / large softboard |
| 65 kg | 60 L+ | 8-ft softboard / mini-mal |
| 80 kg | 75 L+ | 8–9 ft softboard / longboard |

When in doubt, go bigger — a little extra float flattens the learning curve a lot.

## 2. Board types explained

| Type | Length | Traits | Who / use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Softboard** | 7'0"–9'0" | Soft deck, very floaty, doesn't hurt when you fall | A complete beginner's first board, whitewater & standing practice |
| **Longboard** | 9'0"+ | Stable, early take-off, smooth glide | Want to cruise, works even in small surf |
| **Funboard / mini-mal** | 7'0"–8'6" | Longboard-ish float + shorter agility | The first step from soft to hard board |
| **Fish** | 5'6"–6'6" | Wide and flat, speed even in small surf | The next board once you can stand and want to turn |
| **Shortboard** | 5'8"–6'4" | Low volume, turns fast | Advanced only — **beginners, don't buy** |

The route in one line: **softboard → funboard/longboard → fish → shortboard**. Most people spend their first six months between softboard and funboard.

## 4. By use case

- **Never surfed / just practicing standing in waist-high whitewater** → an 8-ft softboard (Odysea, Wavestorm, Olaian)
- **Can ride straight, want fewer wipeouts and earlier take-offs** → a longboard, or the Torq TET soft-deck-hard-core
- **Bored of the softboard, ready for a hard board but still nervous** → a funboard / mini-mal (Torq Mod Fun, NSP Funboard)
- **Can catch unbroken waves and want to start turning** → a fish (the next board, not your first)

## 5. Three traps beginners fall into

1. **Buying a shortboard right away** — not enough float, can't catch waves, most demoralizing.
2. **Choosing on looks and brand alone** — length and volume first, good looks later.
3. **Buying too little volume** — thinking "I'll improve," then suffering through the whole beginner phase. Bigger beats smaller.

## 6. Where to buy in Japan

- **Local surf shop**: you can hold the board and get a staff pick for your height and weight — the safest route.
- **Used (Mercari / Yahoo Auctions)**: plenty of softboards and used Torq/NSP. A practice first board doesn't need to be new; check for water intake, cracks, and repair marks.
- **Decathlon (Olaian)**: the cheapest new entry line — if you're on a budget, this is plenty to start with.
