You can surf year-round in Japan, but water temperature swings a lot. Get the wetsuit wrong and you'll either shiver your way out early or overheat. This guide helps you choose by **water temperature**.

## 1. How a wetsuit keeps you warm

It doesn't keep water out — it lets a thin layer of water sit between the suit and your skin, and your body heat warms that layer into insulation. So:

- **It must fit snugly.** Too big and it constantly flushes cold water — basically useless.
- **Thickness (mm) determines warmth.** Written like `5/3mm`: the first number is the torso, the second is the arms/legs (thinner there for mobility).

## 2. Water temp / season chart (Honshu coast)

| Season | Approx. water temp | Recommended thickness | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peak summer Jul–Sep | 24–28°C | none / rashguard | boardshorts + rashguard, or 2mm short |
| Spring & autumn May–Jun / Oct–Nov | 18–23°C | 3/2mm | full suit (long sleeve) |
| Early winter Dec | 15–18°C | 4/3mm | full + optional hood |
| Deep winter Jan–Mar | 10–15°C | 5/3mm | full + hood + gloves + booties |

Shonan, Chiba and Shizuoka roughly follow this; Miyazaki runs warmer, so drop half a tier. **Always go by the actual measured water temp on the day** — each spot's detail page shows live water temperature.

## 3. Full vs spring — how to choose

| Type | Notes | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Full suit (long sleeve)** | Covers the whole body, warmest | Spring/autumn/winter, the most versatile first suit |
| **Spring (short sleeve/leg)** | Forearms and shins exposed | Early summer, water in the low 20s°C |
| **Vest / long-john combo** | Separates for adjustment | Cool summer days, fine-tuning |
| **Rashguard** | No warmth, sun & rub protection only | Peak summer, or as a layer under a wetsuit |

**What to buy first:** if you buy only one, get a **3/2mm full suit** in Japan — it covers the long spring and autumn windows. Add a 5/3mm later for deep winter.

## 4. Zip position (worth knowing later)

- **Back zip**: easy on and off, beginner-friendly, but flushes a bit more water.
- **Chest zip**: warmer and less flushing, a little harder to get on — the current standard.

Don't overthink it as a beginner; a back zip is easier to start with.

## 5. Common beginner questions

- **Can I size up?** No. Loose means constant cold flushing — the more you wear it, the colder you get. Snug but not so tight it hampers paddling.
- **Inside or out?** A rubber (smooth) side out is more windproof, but most suits go lined-side-in — follow the label.
- **How often to replace?** Cared for, it lasts several seasons. Rinse with fresh water, dry in the shade, no direct sun or washing-machine spin — the rubber degrades.

## 6. Where to buy in Japan

- **Local surf shop**: trying it on matters most; fit makes a huge comfort difference.
- **Brands to consider**: O'Neill, Rip Curl, Billabong, Xcel, and Japanese makers like AXXE CLASSIC / SEA DAYS — domestic brands have a strong reputation for thick winter suits.
- **Used**: thin summer suits are fine; buy winter suits new (old, hardened rubber is noticeably colder).
