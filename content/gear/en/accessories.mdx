Once the board and wetsuit are sorted, the remaining small items don't cost much — but skip them and you won't stay comfortable in the water. Here they are, by priority.

## 1. Sun protection: the most overlooked surf damage

Glare off the water doubles your UV exposure. Two hours surfing unprotected and sunburn is routine.

- **Pick a water/sweat-resistant, high-SPF sunscreen** — SPF50+ is the safe bet.
- **Mineral (zinc oxide) is more water-resistant and gentler**; the downside is it goes on white. The face sticks Japanese surfers use (surf sunscreen / zinc stick) go on cheeks and nose like a crayon and last a whole session.
- **Apply 20 minutes before paddling out** so it sets into a film; ears, back of the neck, and tops of the feet are the easy misses.
- Choose a **reef-safe** formula — better for the ocean.

## 2. Leash

Ties the board to your ankle so it doesn't wash away and hit someone when you fall, and you don't have to swim back for it every time. **A beginner must-have — a safety item.**

- **Length ≈ board length**: an 8-ft board pairs with roughly an 8-ft leash.
- **Thickness**: thin for small surf (light, low drag), thick for big surf (strong). For beginner small surf, standard thickness is fine.
- Strap it to your **back foot** ankle, cord facing back, so it doesn't tangle while paddling.

## 3. Wax / deck pad

Keeps your feet from slipping on the board.

<Figure
  src="/gear/wax-temp.jpg"
  alt="Surf wax sorted by water temperature"
  caption="Wax comes in temperature grades: the colder the water, the softer the wax (Cold → Cool → Warm → Tropical)."
/>

- **Wax**: match it to water temp — `Cold`, `Cool`, `Warm`, `Tropical`. The colder the water, the softer the wax. Wrong wax either won't grip or melts off. Softboards usually don't need wax.
- **Deck pad**: stuck on the tail, common on shortboards; longboards/softboards generally don't need one.
- One block of wax chosen by season is plenty for a beginner.

## 4. Other items, as needed

| Item | When you need it |
|---|---|
| **Hood / gloves / booties** | Deep winter, water below 15°C |
| **Earplugs (Surf Ears, etc.)** | Frequent surfing, to prevent "surfer's ear" (bony ear-canal growth) |
| **Nose clip** | People who roll a lot and get water up the nose |
| **Rashguard** | Peak-summer sun and rub protection, or as a layer under a wetsuit |
| **Changing poncho** | Changing in the parking lot — common at Japanese beaches |
| **Dry bag / key box** | For car keys and phone, so you don't leave them on the sand |

## 5. A beginner's minimum kit

If you're getting only the essentials, one set is:

1. **Board** (softboard or funboard) → see [Beginner Surfboards](/en/gear/beginner-boards)
2. **Wetsuit** (start with a 3/2mm full) → see [How to Choose a Wetsuit](/en/gear/wetsuit)
3. **Leash** (matched to board length)
4. **Wax** (skip for a softboard)
5. **Water-resistant high-SPF sunscreen + a zinc face stick**

Add the rest gradually by season and frequency. Don't spend on flashy accessories up front — put the budget into a well-fitting board and wetsuit, because those two decide how good your sessions feel.
